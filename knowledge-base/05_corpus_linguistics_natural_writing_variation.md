# Document 05 — Corpus linguistics and natural writing variation

## Purpose

Use corpora as evidence of situated writing practices, not as phrase banks or universal style templates.

## Corpus concepts

A corpus is a structured collection of texts sampled for a stated purpose. Interpretations depend on sampling, metadata, annotation, genre, discipline, proficiency, assessment context, and access. Frequency is evidence about a population and register, not a command for an individual writer.

## Relevant resources and evidence

### BAWE

The British Academic Written English corpus contains assessed undergraduate and taught-postgraduate writing across disciplines. Inclusion was based on disciplinary assessment, not solely on mother-tongue status [R30]. Genre-family and multidimensional analyses show variation by discipline, level, and task [R28, R29].

### MICUSP and student corpora

Student writing corpora support analysis of citation, reporting verbs, lexical bundles, and disciplinary variation. Nesi’s BAWE study shows that citation patterns are broader and more discipline-sensitive than simplified writing guides imply [R32].

### Published research corpora

Research-article corpora support analysis of metadiscourse, stance, rhetorical moves, and disciplinary identity [R21–R24, R31, R42]. These are not interchangeable with assessed student assignments.

### L1/L2 and multilingual corpora

L1/L2 writing differs in lexical bundles and syntactic development, but patterns reflect proficiency, educational context, task, and discipline [R27, R35, R38, R39]. Editing must not treat a corpus norm as a “native” endpoint.

## How the humaniser may use corpus evidence

1. Use corpus findings to generate alternatives when a phrase is awkward or collocationally unusual.
2. Compare candidate wording within the inferred discipline and genre.
3. Preserve local author preference when it is grammatical and meaningful.
4. Use concordance-style contextual reasoning: evaluate the full clause and neighbouring sentences.
5. Avoid copying distinctive wording from corpus sources.
6. Record uncertainty when discipline or genre is unknown.

## Example

A writer uses “the results indicate” repeatedly in a Results section. Corpus evidence does not justify replacing every occurrence with “show,” “demonstrate,” and “reveal.” First classify function: if each sentence reports a different statistical result, repetition may be appropriate. If several sentences repeat the same conclusion, compress the content rather than synonymise verbs.

## Limitations

Corpora are sampled, often copyrighted or restricted, and may underrepresent unpublished or non-dominant varieties. Frequency does not equal quality. A corpus-based alternative can still be wrong for the writer’s field, audience, or claim.
