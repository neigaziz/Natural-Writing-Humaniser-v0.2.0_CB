# Document 09 — Text analysis, diagnostic, and rewriting procedures

## End-to-end procedure

### Step 1 — Understand the original

Infer or ask for purpose, central argument, genre, audience, discipline, formality, section, and desired intervention intensity. Record constraints: preserve citations, numbers, equations, terminology, first person, or paragraph structure.

### Step 2 — Build a semantic and rhetorical representation

For each sentence, identify:

- proposition and key entities;
- evidential status: observation, source report, interpretation, hypothesis, or limitation;
- rhetorical role: context, claim, evidence, explanation, contrast, qualification, implication;
- stance and commitment;
- links to neighbouring sentences.

For each paragraph, identify its controlling function and information progression.

### Step 3 — Diagnose only consequential issues

Inspect lexical precision, collocations, redundancy, syntax, paragraph development, transitions, evidence integration, stance, voice, and genre fit. Do not label the text “AI-generated.” Record evidence for each proposed edit.

### Step 4 — Create an editorial plan

Classify each span as **retain**, **clarify**, **rebuild**, **compress**, **expand only if logically necessary**, or **flag**. Prioritise semantic and factual safeguards over stylistic variation.

### Step 5 — Transform contextually

Edit at the smallest level that solves the problem. Rebuild a sentence when word substitution cannot repair the relation. Restructure paragraphs only when the argument order is genuinely weak. Preserve the writer’s perspective and technical vocabulary.

### Step 6 — Validate

Compare source and revision for meaning, claims, numbers, terminology, citations, equations, modality, paragraph function, and voice. If ambiguity remains, disclose it.

## Diagnostic matrix

| Dimension | Questions | Permitted action |
|---|---|---|
| Lexis | Is the word vague, inflated, collocationally awkward, or technically wrong? | Replace precisely; preserve term if valid. |
| Syntax | Is the relation hidden or the construction repetitive/overloaded? | Rebuild, combine, or split with meaning check. |
| Cohesion | Are references and relations clear? | Add or revise links only when logically supported. |
| Coherence | Does each sentence advance the paragraph? | Reorder or compress only if paragraph logic is preserved. |
| Evidence | Is a source integrated and accurately qualified? | Adjust reporting verb/placement; do not invent support. |
| Stance | Is certainty appropriate? | Calibrate only to evidence and writer intent. |
| Voice | Does the revision sound like the writer? | Restore perspective and remove generic phrasing. |
| Genre | Does the passage fit its section and audience? | Apply mode-specific conventions. |

## Ambiguity protocol

If a source sentence is ambiguous, do not silently choose a substantive interpretation. Either preserve the ambiguity if harmless or provide the most conservative revision and flag the issue in the meaning-preservation note.

## Prohibited transformations

- synonym replacement applied uniformly;
- random sentence-length variation;
- deliberate grammar mistakes;
- invented citations, evidence, examples, or personal experiences;
- changing numbers, equations, units, or statistical results;
- upgrading “may” to “does,” “associated with” to “causes,” or “suggests” to “demonstrates” without evidence;
- rewriting for detector evasion.
