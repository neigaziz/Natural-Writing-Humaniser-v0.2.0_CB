# Document 04 — Characteristics of natural human-written text

## Purpose

Define the target quality positively, without equating naturalness with informality, error, or maximal stylistic novelty.

## Core definition

Natural writing is functional writing: wording, syntax, information order, stance, evidence, and voice are appropriate to purpose, audience, genre, discipline, and writer. It can be conventional, repetitive, formal, dense, or highly structured when those choices are motivated.

## Evidence-based dimensions

### Lexis

Human academic prose uses discipline-specific terms, conventional bundles, collocations, and controlled lexical variation [R25–R27, R36, R38]. Repetition of a key term can maintain reference; synonym replacement can create false distinctions.

**Rule:** preserve technical labels and central entities; revise vague, inflated, or locally awkward wording only when the meaning remains stable.

### Syntax

Academic writing uses multiple clause and phrase structures. Complexity is multidimensional and should not be reduced to sentence length or subordination counts [R36, R37, R39].

**Rule:** simplify overloaded syntax and rebuild unclear relations, but retain necessary complexity and passive voice when process or result is the focus.

### Discourse

Human writers construct progression through entity continuity, new information, rhetorical roles, and reader guidance [R21, R33, R34]. Cohesion is not a list of connectors; coherence depends on the relation among propositions.

**Rule:** revise the paragraph’s argument map before changing transitions.

### Stance and engagement

Writers position claims with hedges, boosters, reporting verbs, evaluative language, self-mention, and reader-oriented resources [R21–R24, R31, R32]. Their distribution is disciplinary and genre-specific.

**Rule:** calibrate commitment to evidence and preserve the author’s position; do not globally increase or decrease hedging.

### Citation and evidence

Citations perform functions such as support, contrast, definition, method, background, critique, and source positioning [R32, R42].

**Rule:** retain citations and preserve their source relation. Do not add references or claim that a source supports a proposition unless verified.

### Voice

Voice emerges from recurring choices in perspective, evaluation, rhythm, and positioning within a community [R24, R35]. It is not a fixed fingerprint and must not be manufactured through odd vocabulary or errors.

**Rule:** preserve the writer’s intended perspective and level of formality.

## What not to infer

- Formal vocabulary does not imply AI generation.
- Correct grammar does not imply AI generation.
- A passive sentence is not automatically weak.
- A repeated transition is not automatically bad.
- Non-native English is not evidence of low intellectual quality or artificial authorship.
- Human writing is not defined by randomness or imperfection.
