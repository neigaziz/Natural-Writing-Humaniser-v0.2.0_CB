# Document 07 — Evidence-based rules for natural writing transformation

## Rule hierarchy

1. Preserve meaning, factual status, numbers, equations, citations, and scientific terminology.
2. Preserve genre, discipline, audience, and authorial perspective.
3. Repair grammatical errors and ambiguous syntax when the intended meaning is recoverable.
4. Improve argument progression, evidence integration, and paragraph coherence.
5. Reduce unnecessary repetition, vague wording, inflated language, and mechanical transitions.
6. Introduce lexical or syntactic variation only when it serves clarity or emphasis.
7. Never optimise for AI-detector scores or claim human authorship.

## Category A — lexical editing

- Replace vague words with precise words only when context identifies the intended meaning.
- Remove inflated phrases such as “a significant amount of” when a simpler accurate form is available, but preserve technical terms and legitimate emphasis.
- Improve collocations using discipline-appropriate alternatives.
- Avoid synonym substitution when it changes scope, certainty, causal force, or technical meaning.
- Retain repeated key terms when they stabilise reference.
- Flag a missing concept instead of filling it with generic academic language.

## Category B — syntactic editing

- Rebuild awkward sentences around the actual semantic relation.
- Combine sentences when they express one proposition with redundant repetition.
- Separate sentences when coordination hides distinct claims or overloads working memory.
- Use active/passive voice according to focus, agency, and disciplinary convention.
- Vary openings only when several consecutive sentences have the same information structure.
- Do not create complexity or shortness for its own sake.

## Category C — discourse editing

- Identify each sentence’s role: claim, context, evidence, explanation, contrast, qualification, implication, or transition.
- Make topic continuity and new information visible.
- Replace repeated connectors with direct logical relations only when the relation remains explicit.
- Integrate evidence with interpretation rather than listing citations.
- Preserve distinctions between source report, writer interpretation, and observed result.
- Do not force every paragraph into a universal template.

## Category D — academic editing

- Calibrate hedging to study design, evidence, uncertainty, and discipline.
- Preserve statistical and methodological details.
- Distinguish association from causation, observation from explanation, and hypothesis from finding.
- Preserve reporting verbs that encode source stance.
- Avoid unsupported generalisation and invented references.
- Flag questionable claims separately.

## Category E — authorial voice

- Retain intended perspective and level of formality.
- Preserve legitimate first-person or impersonal choices.
- Avoid inserting personal experience, opinion, or confidence not present in the source.
- Avoid flattening valid L2 or multilingual writing into a generic “native” voice.
- Use the writer’s recurring terms unless they are ambiguous or technically wrong.

## Category F — natural-language quality

- Reduce formulaic openings and conclusions only when they are repetitive or empty.
- Remove duplicated transitions and redundant three-part lists.
- Prefer direct expression over inflated nominalisations when meaning is preserved.
- Maintain an organic relationship between sentence rhythm and argument structure.
- Do not introduce deliberate mistakes, slang, unusual words, or random variation.

## Change threshold

A proposed edit is permitted only if at least one of the following is true:

- it fixes an objective grammatical or punctuation error;
- it resolves a recoverable ambiguity;
- it removes meaning-free redundancy;
- it improves a demonstrably weak logical relation;
- it increases semantic precision without changing evidential force;
- it improves genre or audience fit;
- it preserves the writer’s meaning while making the sentence more idiomatic.

If no threshold is met, retain the original wording.
