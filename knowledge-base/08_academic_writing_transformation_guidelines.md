# Document 08 — Academic writing transformation guidelines

## Default mode

When no mode is specified, use natural, fluent, precise academic English. “Academic” does not mean inflated, impersonal, maximally formal, or uniformly hedged.

## Mode controls

- **Academic research paper:** compact argumentation, source integration, discipline-aware stance, section-specific precision.
- **Dissertation/thesis:** allow explicit signposting and chapter-level navigation; preserve pedagogic development.
- **Literature review:** synthesise sources by themes, methods, evidence, agreement, disagreement, and gaps; avoid citation lists.
- **Scientific manuscript:** protect methods, variables, numbers, statistical terms, units, equations, and result–interpretation boundaries.
- **Research proposal:** distinguish established knowledge, gap, aims, hypotheses, planned methods, feasibility, and expected contribution.
- **Professional writing:** prioritise purpose, action, audience, concision, and appropriate courtesy.
- **Technical writing:** define terms, preserve procedures and constraints, and make dependencies explicit.
- **General English:** prioritise clarity, idiomatic use, and audience fit without imposing academic phrasing.
- **Conversational writing:** preserve natural interaction, warmth, and directness; avoid academic hedging unless intended.
- **Creative writing:** preserve voice, imagery, rhythm, viewpoint, and deliberate deviation; do not normalise stylistic choices.

## Section-specific safeguards

### Introduction and literature review

Preserve the distinction among background, prior findings, critique, gap, and contribution. Use reporting verbs that match source stance. Do not convert a gap into an unsupported claim of novelty.

### Methods

Do not remove repeated technical terms if they identify a variable, procedure, or model. Keep sequence, sample, software, parameter, and statistical detail. Improve readability through clause packaging and headings, not decorative synonymisation.

### Results

Keep numbers, confidence intervals, p-values, effect sizes, units, model names, and direction of effects exactly unchanged. Separate what was observed from what it may mean.

### Discussion

Distinguish interpretation from result, association from causation, and evidence from speculation. Preserve limitations and boundary conditions. Avoid adding explanations absent from the source.

### Abstract and conclusion

Compress without changing claim scope. Do not add generic statements such as “This study provides valuable insights” unless the source contains a specific contribution that can be stated precisely.

## Default output

1. **Revised Text** — complete, clean text with no inline commentary.
2. **Editorial Notes** — concise principal improvements.
3. **Verification Notes** — ambiguities, factual concerns, or unresolved details requiring review.

If the user requests only the rewritten text, output only the revised text.
